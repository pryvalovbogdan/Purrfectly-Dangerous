# Sprite spec

All art is generated by `Tools/sprites/build.py` from the modules in `Tools/sprites/characters/`.
Pixel art, one shared palette (`palette.py`), nearest-neighbour filtering, 1 world unit per pixel.

Outputs per animation:
- frames in `App/Resources/Assets.xcassets/Sprites.spriteatlas/<sprite>_<anim>_<n>.imageset`
- `App/Resources/Sprites.json` — size, anchor, frame count, fps and loop flag (read by `SpriteLibrary`)
- `Tools/sprites/out/sheets/<sprite>_<anim>.png`, `out/preview/<sprite>_<anim>.gif` and
  `out/contact/<sprite>.png` for review (not committed)

Sizes are frame sizes; the figure inside is smaller and anchored at its feet (`cx`, `ground`). Enemies face left in the atlas.

`ghostDash` and `superPounce`; `spark_star|ember|feather` for the burst around the kitten (ember is a curl of

Per theme (`forest`, `frost`, `haunted`, `citadel`): `bg_<theme>_sky` (16×200, stretched across the screen) and
`bg_<theme>_sky_top`; `bg_<theme>_puffs|clouds|far|near|bushes` (320 wide, tiling horizontally, standing on the
ground line); `ground_<theme>` (32×48), `ground_<theme>_earth` (32×36, stacked below it; it must tile vertically and continue from the ground tile's bottom row; the forest draws it as deep grey cobbles rather than reusing the tile's lower part) and `ground_<theme>_fill`; `ravine_<theme>` (the
left wall of a ravine, mirrored for the right one), `ravine_<theme>_fill` (stretched between them; citadel's is
a lava pool) and `ravine_<theme>_deep`. Forest terrain (`characters/terrain.py`) is tiled to a contract so no seam can form. Units are world units, y up, `G` = the
ground line, `S` = a piece's walk surface. All five sprites use anchor (0, 0) when placed by their bottom-left corner.
- `terrain_forest_top`: frame top on `S` (node y = `S` − 16), tiled with `tileOffsets` across the piece, inset 8 at an
  end that gets an end cap, extended 8 under a taller neighbour, flush where a neighbour has the same height.
- `terrain_forest_fill`: the same x span, behind the top tiles; tile bottoms on the grid `G` − 48 + 16k, from the
  lowest needed up to the one holding `S` − 16 (its overshoot is hidden by the opaque top tiles). The fill's 8-row
  cobble rows end on a stone's dark rim at row 15, and `ground_forest_earth` starts on a stone's light top, so the
  fill stacks straight onto the earth with no half stones.
- `terrain_forest_edge`: at an end whose neighbour is lower, frame [x0, x0 + 8) with its top on `S`, mirrored with
  frame [x1 − 8, x1) on the right. Columns 1-7 are opaque, so the inset top/fill tiles run under it. Col 0 holds only
  the grass lip, which overhangs by 1 px, and some tufts and a root.
- `terrain_forest_wall`: under each edge, in the same columns, tiled upward from the lower neighbour's surface (lowest
  tile bottom on it) until a tile reaches `S` − 16. It is drawn behind the edge. Its stone ends are 8-row rows like
  the fill's, so heights and depths in multiples of 8 keep them in line.
- A raised piece is drawn just behind the ground strip, so the base turf passes in front of its foot. For a dip,
  the ground strip is hidden above the floor over [x0 − 8, x1 + 8) (not by cutting the texture). The upper ground
  gets a mirrored edge on [x0 − 8, x0) and an edge on [x1, x1 + 8), with walls down to the floor. The floor's top
  and fill span [x0 − 1, x1 + 1) and fill down to `G` − 48.
- Pieces are at least 24 wide. A piece 16 wide is just its two end columns, with no top or fill tiles.
The forest ravine is a waterfall pit (`characters/waterfall.py`, after `docs/concept/Orange Cat Waterfall Tileset.png`).
Pit [x0, x1), `G` = ground line, y up, pool surface `P` = `G` − 40, `a` = x0 + 8 and `b` = x1 − 8 (between the wall faces).
Positions are node positions with anchor (0, 0); "top on y" means node y = y − height. Back to front:
1. `ravine_forest_back` across [x0, x1) (`tileOffsets`), tops on `G`, `G` − 16, ... down to `P` − 8.
2. `ravine_forest_falls` across [a, b), tops on `G`, `G` − 16, ... while the top is above `P` (the overshoot hides under the pool).
3. `ravine_forest_falls_edge` at x = a and mirrored at [b − 4, b), in the same rows, over the curtain.
4. `ravine_forest_lip` across [a, b), top on `G`.
5. Water across [a, b): `ravine_forest_surface` top on `P`, `ravine_forest_pool` top on `P` − 8, `ravine_forest_deep` tops on
   `P` − 32, `P` − 48, ..., and in tall pits `ravine_forest_deep_fade` as the bottom 32 units on screen.
6. `ravine_forest_splash` across [a, b), top on `P` + 8 (it overlaps the curtain's foot and the surface crest).
7. Walls at x0 and mirrored at [x1 − 8, x1): tops on `G` − 24, `G` − 40, ... to the screen bottom, alternating `ravine_forest`,
   `ravine_forest_b`, `ravine_forest`, ... Every animated piece shows the same frame index at any moment.
8. `ravine_forest_vines` at x0 + 5 and mirrored at [x1 − 11, x1 − 5), top on `G` − 14 (it hangs out from under the rim). More
   strands may go lower on the walls of deep pits, but keep them above `P`.
9. `ravine_forest_rim` at x0 and mirrored at [x1 − 12, x1), top on `G`.
The rim, walls and edges are opaque, so they cover whatever is under the pit. Pits are at least 32 wide.

`island` is placed like `bridge` (frame top = platform y) and is drawn once, not tiled.

## How characters are drawn

Characters are posed, not painted. The hero is an upright chibi cat drawn by `characters/hero.py`'s
`chibi(canvas, HeroPose, cx, ground)`: leg and arm angles, torso lean (90 = flat out), cape angle and ripple,
tail angle and curl, eyes and ears; `running(phase)` gives the run cycle. The ghost cats use the four-legged
`cat(canvas, CatPose, cx, ground, coat)`: leg angles (0° = straight down, positive = forward), body
stretch and tilt, tail angle and curl, eyes and ears; `gallop(phase)` gives the run cycle. Humanoids can
still use `draw.humanoid(canvas, Body, Pose, cx, ground)`. `Canvas.outline()` adds the dark 1px outline
afterwards. The forest critters are built from shaded parts in `critters.py` (`shaded` lights each part's top-left
rim and darkens the far rim, `face` draws the big cat eyes with a shine, `ear`, `sparkle`). Death frames come from `vanish()` (flash, then sink or rise while fading), `shatter()` (breaks
into blocks that fly and fall; also used for crumbling platforms) or `topple()`; `see_through()` lowers
opacity for ghosts and fades.
The four world bosses follow `docs/concept/Pixel Boss Atlas_ Feline Realms.png` (reference only) and share
`Tools/sprites/bosskit.py`: each body part is drawn in one base colour inside `with part(canvas, ramp, edge)`,
which rim-lights its top-left, shades its bottom-right and gives it its own inner outline. It also has `blob`
(furry ellipse), `tube`/`bezier` (tails, necks), `crystal`, `acorn`, `flame`, `flame_jet`, `smoke` and `halo`.
The boss hazards are drawn in `characters/boss_hazards.py` with the same helpers and palette (`SQ_`, `GL_`,
`QN_`, `GM_` colours).
Draw facing right; pass `faces_left=True` for enemies. Set `cx`/`ground` on the `Sprite` so the
game anchors the feet, not the frame corner.
